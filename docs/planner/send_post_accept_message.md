---
principles:
  - Post-accept messaging is a separate stage from the original invite.
  - The first message is relationship-first, not a pitch. Just acknowledge the connection and that you look forward to chatting.
  - The direct engagement (the wedge, a concrete point, an ask) comes later — on their reply, or on a follow-up after a couple of quiet days.
do:
  - Pull the post-accept message brief before drafting.
  - Keep it to one or two warm, plain sentences that say it's good to connect and you're looking forward to talking.
  - Follow docs/writing-voice.md (no em dashes, no semicolons, no jargon, no "reach out" openers).
avoid:
  - Do not pitch, ask for a meeting, introduce the product, or include a link in this first message.
  - Do not add a setup question or agenda. Save the real conversation for the reply or the follow-up.
  - Do not re-send the connection-request logic. Do not escalate to email first unless the plan explicitly says so.
writeback:
  - Record the sent post-accept message.
  - Advance cadence to the next checkpoint (wait for a reply; a follow-up that engages directly comes after a couple of quiet days).
